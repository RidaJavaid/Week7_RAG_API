Chunking divides long documents into smaller searchable passages. A sliding-window strategy uses a fixed chunk size and overlap; overlap protects context that crosses boundaries but also creates duplicated text. Semantic-break chunking tries to split near topic or paragraph boundaries so each chunk is more coherent. The best strategy depends on document structure and should be measured using retrieval metrics rather than chosen only by intuition. BM25 is a sparse lexical ranking algorithm based on term frequency, inverse document frequency, and document-length normalization. Term frequency rewards repeated query terms but BM25 saturates that reward so repetition does not grow without limit. Inverse document frequency gives more weight to terms that occur in fewer documents. The b parameter controls document-length normalization and k1 controls term-frequency saturation. Hybrid retrieval can combine BM25 and dense retrieval. Reciprocal Rank Fusion, or RRF, combines ranked lists using reciprocal rank positions and does not require raw BM25 and dense scores to share the same scale. A reranker receives a smaller candidate list and scores query-passage pairs more carefully. Cross-encoders are usually more accurate than bi-encoder retrieval but slower because every query-candidate pair must be processed jointly. LLM-as-judge reranking can use richer instructions but is generally more expensive, slower, and potentially less deterministic. Retrieval evaluation can use Hit Rate at k, Mean Reciprocal Rank, precision, recall, NDCG, and latency. Hit Rate at k checks whether at least one relevant item appears in the top k. MRR rewards placing the first relevant result near the top.
